Module 2 · Guest frequency

Guest frequency lab

The public goal is more visits from the same guests, and the membership tiers are the planned way to get there. This lab shows the trade-off that matters: how much contact helps, and where it starts to push guests away. Every assumption is a slider you can change.

Contact frequency

Where more messages stop helping

The model is deliberately simple, so the shape of the trade-off is easy to see and challenge. It is not a forecast.

Model: visits = base × (1 + lift × (1 − e−0.9c) − penalty × max(0, c − cap)). Opt-outs = members × (0.002c + 0.01 × max(0, c − cap)). c is messages per guest per week.

Membership tiers

Do the planned tiers pay for themselves?

Compares each tier with the same guest paying per visit. Positive means the membership brings in more revenue than pay-per-visit would. Negative means the tier gives up revenue, which is cannibalization, and it shows up early.

Tier values are illustrative. Real prices and credits are not public.

Lapse and second-visit triggers

When to send something, and when to send a person

Each trigger has a cap, so no guest receives the same trigger twice in a month.

Second-visit nudge

Automated

First visit in the last 14 days, no return yet. Send one offer for a weekday visit. Stop after two sends.

Cap: 1 per 30 days · Cost to guest: none · Goal: second visit within 21 days

Win-back offer

Automated

No visit in 60 to 90 days, and the guest has not opted out. Offer a credit for a visit in the next two weeks. Do not send if the guest visited in the last 30 days.

Cap: 1 per 30 days · Goal: visit within 14 days

Avid member drop-off

Human-led

Visits fell by 40% or more month over month for an avid member. A venue team member sends a personal note or invites them to a clinic. No automated offer.

Owner: venue guest team · Logged for review

Family visit reminder

Extra review

Reminder for a group booking the guest has made before. Goes through the youth review group because family groups can include minors.

Owner: marketing · Review: youth group
